# Introduction
The purpose of this blog is to talk about my experience with meditation, explain what it is, give observations and to also give some tips to anyone reading this who perhaps wants to learn more. 

I've been "meditating" on and off since University. I started off with guided meditations and there is a video of me talking about one called the view from afar. I believe this is actually what got me to look into this topic more deeply. I then started to do breathwork such as Wim Hof and Tummo but albeit I was only really doing it to get a bit of a high. It also really helps with recovery and I'll do breathwork if I'm going through an injury. Around that point I realised that I've been meditating for a lot longer than I thought. Personally, I would count controlled activities such as running, weightlifting and walking as a form of meditation although it needs to be without any dopaminergic stimuli such as music or videos. While it's not meditation in the most practical sense, there will be moments where you're doing something almost on autopilot without any other thoughts. 

# What is meditation?
My definition of meditation is to focus on one thing and one thing only. People refer this thing as the "anchor". For example, as you're reading this post, you're breathing manually and you can feel each breath going in and out. You can feel your stomach moving in and out. You can perhaps hear your breath, whether it's going fast or slow. You can stop breathing if you choose to do so trusting that your body will find the next best time to take the next breath (due to air hunger). 

Another type of anchor is focus on a body part such as your right foot. Where it's touching, how it feels, whether there's any soreness or pain, and slowly the more you focus on your foot it'll start to go numb, and it'll be as if it was never there.

If you're a sweat, your anchor is nothing. You think of nothing. It's hard to think of nothing but you focus on thinking of nothing. I'll explain more about this type of anchor later.

Now, the anchor you choose isn't really important. While you focus on your anchor, thoughts will start to come in. You'll be focusing on your breath and you remember a conversation, an idea, an emotion, a feeling. Those thoughts will ruminate, expand, develop, twist and turn until you forget that you were meditating. The second you remember, you go back to your anchor. That is to meditate. 

At the start, it'll be impossible. You may focus on your anchor for 30 seconds and spend the next 10 minutes deep in thought. But meditation is also training your mind to identify these thoughts happening and to return back to the anchor. The more you do it, the better you are at identifying when your mind is wandering and you get better at returning to the anchor. Sometimes, you'll be able to almost feel a thought about to develop and you can instantly go back to your anchor. 

## Are thoughts bad
Between the point of neuroticism and being in a meditative state, I've noticed personally there is a point where the quality of my thoughts are extremely good. I'll get ideas, solutions to problems and also form questions that I would not have done if I was actively trying to think. It's the state at which I'm trying not to think where my best thoughts come. I don't know why this happens, it just does and I won't try to do some sort of psuedoscience explanation either. 

# Observations
My meditation journey has had a few observations over the years. I personally believe I've become less neurotic. Part of that could be that my brain is still developing and it's naturally becoming more quiet. When I say neurotic, I mean my brain never fully switches off. When I go to bed, I have multiple thoughts, emotions and songs running rampant and the sound and electricity doesn't die down and I can't fall asleep. Insomnia has affected me ever since I was a child and I remember days where I would be looking at the curtain all night trying to sleep and seeing sunlight coming through realising it's the morning and a new day has begun. Meditation has been the only thing which has been able to control my thoughts, observe my thinking and to put my brain on mute so I can fall asleep. Therefore 99% of my deep meditation sessions are in bed. 

The next observation I've made is I've recently (past 6 months or so) been questioning the things I'm doing and why I'm doing them. I remember working on something and actively thinking to myself "why am I doing this?". As silly as it sounds, I've never actually asked myself that question when it came to work, career, education. I've always gone with the flow in life which perhaps is a bit strange but an observation nontheless.

# Stages of meditation 
Here I want to talk about the stages of meditation. I think meditating is made of multiple parts, some parts I may not even be aware off and may have to discover with more practice. 

You can't automatically go into a meditative state. Imagine a car going in gear 6 and you want to go into gear 1. You cannot shift the gearbox to first gear. The gearbox will eitehr explode or the car will not let you do it. Same with the brain. If you want to go from an elevated thinking state to a meditative state you need to realise there are some stages. All stages I think are equally as difficult.

The first stage is is actually starting. You need to find a position i.e laying down, eyes closed, deciding on the anchor etc. The second stage is dealing with thoughts while focusing on the anchor. This may be considered the hardest part at the start but as time goes on it'll get better. The stage after that is the stage in which thoughts become of better quality because you filter out the random ones and it's extremely easy to get lost at this stage. The stage after that is actually having a good amount of time focusing on the anchor. A side quest of the stage is sleeping which is completely fine if you want that. If not then you start to wake up, collect your mind and go about your day with almost a clearer mind. 

There is a point where once you've gone past the distrating thoughts and are able to think of absolutely nothing for an unknown amount of time. I feel drawn to this state although I don't know exactly what it is. I have an innate desire to return to this state. It's not like sleep because I'm unconscious when I'm alseep. You are aware of the state and can come out of it whenever you please or when your mental endurance runs out and the thoughts come back.
